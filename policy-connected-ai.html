Connected AIRules for AI that can act

A standard for AI assistants and agents connected to your email, files, calendars, accounts or payment systems. It states what access they receive, which actions need a person’s approval, and how spending is limited.

Before you start

  • You need this standard only once an AI tool can take actions rather than only answer questions. If none of your tools can, keep the standard ready for when one does.
  • The governing principle is least access: the access the job requires, and nothing more.
  • Anything that sends, pays, deletes or changes records needs a person’s approval.

Why AI that can act needs its own rules

An AI assistant that reads your email can receive instructions from anyone who sends you an email. This is the essence of prompt injection: text hidden in a message, document or web page that the assistant treats as an instruction. No reliable method of preventing it is known, so the practical defense is to limit what the assistant can do if it is misled, and to require a person’s approval for any action that matters.

The same limits also protect against more ordinary failures, such as an assistant that misunderstands a request, or a process that runs unchecked and accumulates charges.

Connected AI standard · copy and adapt

[Business name]: AI tools that can act

Version [1.0], [date]. Owner: [AI lead].

What this covers

Any AI tool that can read our email, files, calendars or other systems, or can take actions such as sending messages, booking, buying, paying, deleting or changing records. This includes AI agents, assistants connected to our accounts, and browser tools that can act on web pages.

Before a tool is connected

  • Connected AI tools are Level 3 under our tool approval procedure, and need [owner]’s approval.
  • We record what the tool is for, what it needs to reach, and what it must not do.
  • We try it first with limited access, on work where a mistake would have little effect.

Access

  • Each tool receives the least access that the job requires: read-only access, unless it must change data.
  • Connect only the mailboxes, folders, calendars or accounts it needs, not everything the person using it can see.
  • Where the product allows it, use a separate account or restricted permissions for the tool.
  • Never type passwords into an AI tool. Use the product’s own sign-in connection, so access can be removed later.
  • Before connecting a tool to shared files, check that the files’ permissions are correct.

Actions that need a person’s approval every time

  • Sending an email or message outside the business
  • Making or approving a payment, refund or purchase
  • Deleting anything
  • Changing customer, financial or staff records
  • Accepting terms, making a booking, or committing us to anything
  • [Other actions specific to our business]

The person approving must examine the action itself, meaning the exact email or the exact amount, not only the tool’s description of it.

Spending

  • Every AI service billed by usage has a spending limit of [amount] a month, set in the provider’s account so that usage stops rather than only triggering a warning, with an alert at [75%].
  • Tools that can buy things have a limit of [amount] per transaction, where the product allows it.

Plugins and add-ons

Plugins, extensions, connectors and add-ons for AI tools must be approved like any other tool. Install them only from the provider’s official marketplace, or from developers we have checked.

Monitoring

  • [AI lead] reviews what each connected tool has done at least every [month], using its activity log where it has one.
  • Anyone who sees a connected tool do something unexpected should disconnect it, or ask [AI lead] to do so, and report it immediately.
  • [AI lead] keeps written instructions for disconnecting each tool quickly.

Adapting it

  • Watch for tools that combine three things: reading content from outside the business (email, the web), having access to sensitive information, and being able to send or change things. Prompt injection does the most damage where all three are present, so such tools need the tightest limits.
  • Check how the product handles approval. Some tools ask before every action; others offer only a choice between all actions and none. Choose products that let you approve individual actions.
  • Test the off switch. Before you rely on a connected tool, confirm that you know how to disconnect it, and that disconnecting it stops it.
