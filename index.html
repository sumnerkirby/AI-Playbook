The AI Playbook Project is an initiative led by the University of Oklahoma Cybersecurity Clinic. Its purpose is to help small businesses identify, govern, and secure the artificial intelligence tools they use.

Most small businesses now use AI in some form. An employee might use a chat assistant to draft email, and an accounting or customer-service product might have added AI features in a recent update. In both cases the business sends its information to a system it did not build. A common gap is the lack of a record: which tools are in use, what information goes into them, and who is responsible for them.

The playbook addresses this in nine steps. The steps cover assigning responsibility, listing the AI tools in use, setting rules for their use, protecting data, and responding when something goes wrong. The playbook assumes a business with no security staff and no compliance department.

The steps draw on two published sources: the AI Risk Management Framework from the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST), and the OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications. Where a step relies on one of these sources, it quotes the passage verbatim and links to the original, so that you can compare the text with our reading of it.

What this site does not cover

The site is written for businesses of up to 50 people that use ready-made AI tools, or set up their own assistants and automations without writing code. It does not cover the following. Where another source can help, it is listed.

  • Healthcare and wellness. Patient information has its own rules under HIPAA. HHS: Business associates; HHS: Guidance on HIPAA and cloud computing.
  • Education and childcare. Student and children’s information has its own rules. U.S. Department of Education: Protecting student privacy; FTC: Complying with COPPA.
  • Government contracting. Controlled Unclassified Information and export-controlled data must stay in systems approved for them. DoD CIO: CMMC; APEX Accelerators: no-cost help for government contractors.
  • Writing code that uses AI. The site’s library has the OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications, which is written for developers.
  • Businesses of more than 50 people. The NIST AI Risk Management Framework is the fuller framework this playbook draws on.
  • Your wider IT security, such as backups, passwords and devices in general. SBA: Strengthen your cybersecurity; your state’s Small Business Development Center, such as the Oklahoma Small Business Development Centers.
