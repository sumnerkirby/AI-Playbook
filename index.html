AI Playbookfor small businesses

The AI Playbook Project is an initiative led by the University of Oklahoma Cybersecurity Clinic. Its purpose is to help small businesses identify, govern, and secure the artificial intelligence tools they use.

Most small businesses now use AI in some form. An employee might use a chat assistant to draft email, and an accounting or customer-service product might have added AI features in a recent update. In both cases the business sends its information to a system it did not build. The larger risk is usually not any one tool but the lack of a record: which tools are in use, what information goes into them, and who is responsible for them.

The playbook addresses this in nine steps. The steps cover assigning responsibility, listing the AI tools in use, setting rules for their use, protecting data, and responding when something goes wrong. The playbook assumes a business with no security staff and no compliance department.

The steps draw on two published sources: the AI Risk Management Framework from the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST), and the OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications. Where a step relies on one of these sources, it quotes the passage verbatim and links to the original, so that you can compare the text with our reading of it.

The site runs entirely in your web browser. Anything you enter is stored in that browser only and is not sent to a server.

Two ways to begin

Quick start · About 5 minutes

The quick AI check

Six yes-or-no questions identify the situations most likely to cause harm. You then select the everyday tasks in which your business uses AI, and the check sorts each one into three groups: acceptable, needs a check, and stop.

The nine-step playbook

The steps are meant to be done in order. Steps 1 to 3 address the largest risks, so a business that completes only those three has covered the most important ground.
