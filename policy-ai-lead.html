AI leadA role description

A one-page description of the person who looks after AI in your business: what they’re responsible for, what they can decide on their own, and what comes back to you. It makes step 1 of the playbook official.

Before you start

  • The AI lead doesn’t need to be technical. They need to be organised, trusted by the team, and willing to say no.
  • In most small businesses this is part of an existing job rather than a new one. Allow an hour or two a month.
  • Fill in the decision limits with real figures, so everyone knows where the line is.

Why write it down

Writing the role down does more than it might seem. It tells the team who to ask, it gives the person permission to spend time on it, and it makes clear which decisions are theirs and which stay with you. Without that, the role tends to drift: either nobody really does it, or someone does it without the authority to make anything stick.

AI lead role description · copy and adapt

[Business name]: AI lead

Role holder: [name]. Reports to: [owner or manager]. Time: about [two hours] a month. From: [date].

Purpose

The AI lead makes sure [business name] uses AI tools safely and sensibly. They are the first person to ask about anything to do with AI at work.

Responsibilities

  • Keep our list of AI tools up to date, including AI features in software we already pay for.
  • Keep our AI policy current, and make sure everyone has read the latest version.
  • Handle requests for new AI tools, following our tool approval procedure.
  • Check the terms and settings of approved tools, and record what was checked.
  • Be the person staff report AI problems and near misses to, and keep the incident log.
  • Run a short review of our AI use every [three] months, and report back to [owner or manager].
  • Keep reasonably up to date with changes to the tools we use.

Decisions the AI lead can make

  • Approve a new AI tool that won’t receive customer or staff information, won’t be used with customers, can’t take actions on its own, and costs less than [amount] a month.
  • Change settings on approved tools to make them more private or secure.
  • Suspend use of any AI tool straight away if they believe it is causing harm or exposing information.

Decisions that come to [owner or manager]

  • Any AI tool that will receive customer or staff information.
  • Any AI tool customers will interact with directly, such as a chatbot.
  • Any AI tool that can send messages, make payments or change records.
  • Anything costing more than [amount] a month.
  • Any incident that may need to be reported to customers, a regulator or an insurer.

Cover

When [name] is away, [deputy’s name] handles anything urgent, including suspending a tool or dealing with an incident.

Agreed by: [owner]   Date: [date]

Adapting it

  • Set limits that suit your business. An accountancy firm handling client finances will want the owner involved sooner than a design studio using AI for mood boards.
  • Name a deputy. Problems don’t wait for holidays, and the deputy only needs to know how to suspend a tool and who to call.
  • Write it down even if the AI lead is you. It still tells the team who to ask, and it makes you decide where your own limits are.
  • Look at it again once a year, or whenever the person in the role changes.
