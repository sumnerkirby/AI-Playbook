AI leadA role description

A one-page description of the role of the person responsible for AI in your business: what the role covers, which decisions the role holder can make alone, and which come back to you. It formalizes step 1 of the playbook.

Before you start

  • The AI lead does not need a technical background. The role requires someone who is organized, trusted by staff, and willing to refuse a request.
  • In most small businesses the role is part of an existing job, not a new position. Allow an hour or two a month.
  • Fill in the decision limits with actual figures, so that everyone knows where the limits are.

Why write it down

A written role description serves three purposes. It tells staff whom to ask, it gives the role holder permission to spend time on the work, and it states which decisions belong to the role holder and which remain with you. Without a written description, the role tends to lapse: either nobody does the work, or someone does it without the authority to enforce any decisions.

AI lead role description · copy and adapt

[Business name]: AI lead

Role holder: [name]. Reports to: [owner or manager]. Time: about [two hours] a month. From: [date].

Purpose

The AI lead makes sure that [business name] uses AI tools safely and appropriately, and is the first person to ask about any question concerning AI at work.

Responsibilities

  • Keep our list of AI tools up to date, including AI features in software we already pay for.
  • Keep our AI policy current, and make sure that everyone has read the latest version.
  • Handle requests for new AI tools, following our tool approval procedure.
  • Check the terms and settings of approved tools, and record what was checked.
  • Receive reports of AI problems and near misses from staff, and keep the incident log.
  • Run a short review of our AI use every [three] months, and report back to [owner or manager].
  • Stay informed about changes to the tools we use.

Decisions the AI lead can make

  • Approve a new AI tool that will not receive customer or staff information, will not be used with customers, cannot take actions on its own, and costs less than [amount] a month.
  • Change settings on approved tools to make them more private or secure.
  • Suspend use of any AI tool immediately if it appears to be causing harm or exposing information.

Decisions that come to [owner or manager]

  • Any AI tool that will receive customer or staff information.
  • Any AI tool customers will interact with directly, such as a chatbot.
  • Any AI tool that can send messages, make payments or change records.
  • Anything costing more than [amount] a month.
  • Any incident that may need to be reported to customers, a regulator or an insurer.

Cover

When [name] is away, [deputy’s name] handles anything urgent, including suspending a tool or dealing with an incident.

Agreed by: [owner]   Date: [date]

Adapting it

  • Set limits that suit your business. An accounting firm that handles client finances will want the owner involved sooner than a design studio that uses AI for mood boards.
  • Name a deputy. Problems can occur while the AI lead is away. The deputy needs to know only how to suspend a tool and whom to call.
  • Write it down even if the AI lead is you. It still tells staff whom to ask, and it requires you to decide where your own limits are.
  • Review it once a year, and whenever the person in the role changes.
