Tool approvalHow a new AI tool gets approved

A short procedure for deciding whether a new AI tool may be used for work, with a request form for staff to complete. It keeps your list of tools accurate, and it makes approval decisions consistent regardless of who is asked.

Before you start

  • Keep the process quick for low-risk tools. Otherwise, staff will skip it and sign up anyway.
  • The more a tool can see and do, the more checking it receives. The three levels below sort requests accordingly.
  • Every approved tool goes on your list of AI tools, with the date it was checked.

Why have a procedure

The common problem in small businesses is not that staff request too many AI tools, but that they do not request them at all. A short, predictable procedure addresses this. If staff know that a request takes a few days and a few questions, they will ask rather than sign up with a personal account.

A procedure also ensures that two similar requests receive the same answer, regardless of who handles them.

The procedure

Tool approval procedure · copy and adapt

[Business name]: Approving AI tools

Version [1.0], [date]. Owner: [AI lead].

Anyone who wants to use a new AI tool for work, or a new AI feature in software we already use, completes the request form and sends it to [AI lead]. Do not use the tool for work until it has been approved.

How requests are handled

[AI lead] assigns each request to one of three levels, based on the answers on the form.

Level 1: low risk. The tool will not receive customer, staff or confidential information, will not be used with customers, and cannot take actions on our behalf. [AI lead] checks the provider’s terms and privacy settings and decides within [three working days].

Level 2: handles our information. The tool will receive customer, staff or confidential information. As well as the Level 1 checks, [AI lead] obtains answers to our supplier questions (from the provider’s published documents, or by asking the provider), confirms that we can use a business account that we control, and confirms that the provider will not use our data for training. [Owner] makes the final decision.

Level 3: customer-facing, or able to act. Customers will interact with the tool directly, or it can send messages, make payments or change records. As well as the Level 2 checks, we agree how its work will be checked (our human review standard) and what access it gets (our rules for AI that can act). We try it on a small scale first. [Owner] makes the final decision.

Once a decision is made

  • Approved tools are added to our AI list, with their level, the account to use, any conditions, and the date they were checked.
  • The person who made the request is told the decision and any conditions, such as a restriction on customer information.
  • If a request is declined, [AI lead] explains why and suggests an approved alternative where there is one.
  • Approvals are reviewed at each quarterly AI review. A tool may need to be approved again if its terms, its features, or the way we use it change significantly.

The request form

Make the form easy to find, as a shared document or a simple online form. The questions are written so that the answers tell the AI lead which level a request belongs to.

AI tool request form · copy and adapt

AI tool request

Your name: ____________   Date: ____________

  1. What is the tool? Give its name, its website, and the plan or version you would use.
  2. What do you want to use it for? Give an example of a typical task.
  3. Who else would use it?
  4. What information would go into it? Check all that apply.
    ☐ Only public or non-sensitive information
    ☐ Customer names, contact details or account information
    ☐ Staff information
    ☐ Financial information or contracts
    ☐ Information a client has asked us to keep confidential
  5. Will customers see what it produces, or interact with it directly?  ☐ Yes  ☐ No
  6. Will it connect to our email, files, calendar, accounts or other systems?  ☐ Yes  ☐ No
    If yes, which?
  7. Can it take actions on its own, such as sending messages, booking, paying or deleting?  ☐ Yes  ☐ No
  8. What does it cost, and who would pay for it?
  9. Could a tool we already use do this job?

For [AI lead] to complete

Level: ☐ 1  ☐ 2  ☐ 3    Decision: ☐ Approved  ☐ Approved with conditions  ☐ Declined

Conditions: ____________________________________________

Decided by: ____________   Date: ____________   Added to AI list: ☐

Adapting it

  • Question 4 determines most levels. Any box checked below the first one makes a request at least Level 2. A yes to question 5, 6 or 7 makes it Level 3.
  • AI features in existing software count. When a supplier switches on a new AI feature, treat it as a request, even if nobody asked for it.
  • Keep Level 1 simple. If a low-risk request takes weeks, staff will ignore the procedure for every request.
