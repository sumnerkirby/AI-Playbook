Policy template · Step 6 of the playbook · 1–2 pages

Human reviewChecking AI output before use

A short standard that states which AI-assisted work a person must check, what checking involves, and who is responsible. It turns step 6 of the playbook into a procedure staff can follow.

Before you start

  • Base the categories on the work your staff use AI for. Your AI list records this.
  • Define what checking means. Without a definition, a check made under time pressure becomes a glance.
  • Decisions about people, and AI that communicates with customers, each have a separate rule.

Why write it down

Almost everyone agrees that AI output should be checked. The difficulty is that checking can mean anything from careful verification to a quick skim, and a busy person will usually skim. The more often a tool is right, the easier it becomes to stop checking. A written statement of what must be checked, and what checking involves, keeps the practice in place.

Human review standard · copy and adapt

[Business name]: Checking AI-assisted work

Version [1.0], [date]. Owner: [AI lead].

The basic rule

If you send, publish or act on something an AI tool produced or helped to produce, you are responsible for it, exactly as if you had written it yourself.

Work that must always be checked by a person

  • Anything sent to a customer, supplier or member of the public
  • Anything containing figures, prices, dates, quotes or deadlines
  • Anything legal or financial, or about health and safety
  • Anything published under our name, including on social media and our website
  • [Other kinds of work specific to our business]

Work that does not need a formal check

Internal notes, first drafts, brainstorming, and summaries read only by the person who asked for them. Use your judgment: if someone will rely on it, check it.

What checking means

  • Read all of it, not only the beginning.
  • Check every fact, figure, name and date against the original source, not against the AI tool’s summary of it.
  • Check that any document, law, article or link it mentions exists and says what the output claims.
  • Check that it matches our actual prices and policies, and what we have agreed with the customer.
  • Check the tone, and that nothing confidential has been included by mistake.
  • If you cannot check something, remove it, or ask someone who can.

Work that needs a second check

[Contracts, advice to clients, and anything sent to more than 50 people] must also be checked by [name or role] before it goes out.

Decisions about people

AI must not make final decisions about people, including hiring, pay, performance, credit, individual pricing, or whether a customer receives a service. AI may only be used to help prepare information for decisions like these with [owner]’s agreement, and the person making the decision must look at the underlying information themselves.

AI that communicates with customers

Any AI that communicates with customers directly, such as a chatbot on our website, must:

  • make clear to customers that they are communicating with an AI system
  • stay within [the topics it is approved for], and not make commitments about [prices, refunds or delivery dates]
  • give customers an easy way to reach a person
  • have a sample of its conversations read by [name] every [week]

When a mistake gets through

Tell [AI lead]. We will correct it with the customer if necessary, and use it to improve this standard. You will not be disciplined for reporting it.

Adapting it

  • Add your own examples. A specific item, such as quotes sent from the CRM or the monthly client report, is clearer than a general category.
  • Limit the second check. Reserve it for work where a mistake would be costly. Otherwise it will become a formality.
  • If you do not yet use AI with customers, keep that section anyway. It sets the rules before anyone switches on a chatbot.
